Home Runs

Track every circuit from the panel — what's pulled, what's left

The 30-second version

Every circuit on the job gets a row here: name, wire size, which panel it lands in. As you pull them, check them off. The card at the very top of the tab always shows the honest score — how many pulled, how many left — and the office reads the same number you do. Panels and their printable breaker schedules live on this tab too.

The pull card at the top

·

It's the whole job in one line

"X pulled · Y left." Tap the card and it expands into one flat A-to-Z list of every named home run on the job — no digging into a panel or floor first. Not Pulled is listed first because that's your work; every row shows its wire size, panel, and floor so you don't have to open anything.

Two ways to view the list

·

By Floor — the default

Rows grouped by Main / Upper / Basement — plus any areas you add yourself. Detached garage, shop, ADU? Hit + Add Floor / Area and give it its own group instead of shoehorning runs into the wrong floor. Its rows flow into every count and the top pull list like any other. This is where you add new home runs and where most day-to-day checking off happens.

·

By Panel

The same rows grouped by which panel they land in, with a pulled count per panel — use it when you're standing at the panel trimming it out.

Adding home runs

1

By Floor → your floor → add a row

Add the run on the floor it feeds.

2

Name it — the name is what makes it count

"Kitchen counters east", "Master bath GFI". A row with no name doesn't show up in any count — not the top card, not the panel totals. Name it the way you'd say it on the phone.

3

Wire size + panel

Pick the wire size — the breaker comes from the wire automatically. Pick the panel from the dropdown so the schedule and counts know where it lands.

There's no breaker field on the row — a row is just panel, wire, name, status, and the 240V toggle where it applies. Need to correct a breaker count? That happens per panel: hit Edit on the panel's breaker summary card to adjust the breaker groups by hand (amps, poles, count), and use the panel's Space count override if the space count needs a hand-set number. Those are for the exceptions, not the routine.

4

240V on 14/2 or 12/2? Flip the 240V flag

The 240V toggle only shows on 14/2 and 12/2 rows — a 2-wire cable run 240V-only. Flip it and the run counts as a 2-pole everywhere: the panel schedule and the breaker count cards. Every other 240V circuit — 10/3 dryer, 6/3 range — is already a 2-pole straight from its wire size. No flag to remember there.

Big list to enter? Bulk paste

The Bulk paste button takes a pasted list and lands the runs as rows so you're not typing forty names on a phone. Every run has to lead with its wire size — "12/2 Kitchen counters east", "10/3 Dryer" — split by new lines, commas, or pipes. A bare list of names with no wire sizes comes back "0 rows detected" and nothing lands. Use the DEFAULT PANEL dropdown in the modal to assign a panel as you paste, or set panels on the rows after.

Marking pulled

·

Check the row off the day you pull it

The top card, the floor counts, and the panel counts all update from that one check. If it's checked, the office believes it's in the wall — so keep it honest, both directions.

Can't pull a run because the specs aren't in? Set its status to Need Specs instead — the row goes red so nobody mistakes it for forgotten. Every status change stamps who set it and the date right under the row. That stamp is the accountability behind the count.

·

GC keeps asking "how many are pulled?" — send the link

The share row on this tab makes a read-only live page of pull status you can send a GC or builder — real-time pull status for whoever keeps asking. It replaces the daily phone call. They see the numbers as they change; they can't touch the rows.

Panels & panel schedules

·

Add a panel ONCE — you get both pieces

Adding a panel in the Panels section also creates its blank breaker schedule, and adding one on the schedules side adds the name to every row's panel dropdown. You never type the same panel twice.

·

FILL builds the schedule from your home runs

A new schedule stays blank until someone hits FILL — then it lays the breakers out from the home-run rows assigned to that panel. The finished schedule is the printable sheet for the panel cover.

If you've typed a manual breaker count and the rows drift away from it, a "Manual count is stale" banner shows up with a Refresh — tap it and the count catches up to reality instead of quietly lying.

Know what's protected and what isn't. The automatic background re-fill only ever touches a schedule that still matches exactly what FILL wrote last time. The moment you hand-type a circuit, the auto side leaves that panel alone forever — the button just turns into an orange RE-FILL alert. But pressing that button yourself is a different story: the app warns "Fill from home runs? This will REPLACE any circuit names you've typed in this panel." — and it means it. Confirm, and your hand-typed circuits are gone. Don't tap through that confirm unless you're ready to lose the hand edits.

·

The breaker count cards tell you what to order

Each panel's breaker count card totals the breakers its rows need. Overflow the panel — say 46 circuits into a 40-space can — and the card puts up a banner with the fix: how many tandem breakers and quad breakers to order, and a warning if circuits still won't fit even then. Then + Add to PO pushes that exact breaker order onto a purchase order — pick the source, check the line preview, done. No math in the truck.

Deleting never cleans up for you — on purpose

Deleting a panel chip does NOT delete its schedule, and deleting a schedule does NOT remove the panel from the dropdowns. A schedule holds typed circuits and rows may still point at the name — so nothing cascades. Don't delete to "tidy up."

Generator load selection

·

Pick what's on the generator

Check the loads that ride the generator. Select all does the whole list in one tap and flips to Deselect all once everything's on. The readout — "N of M on the generator" — is what lands in front of the homeowner, so make it match reality.

·

Or let the homeowner pick — send them the link

This section can send the homeowner a link to choose their generator loads themselves — they submit it with a signature. Check the response right here to see if they've answered, and resend if the link needs to go out again. If they already submitted, the app asks "Homeowner already submitted. Reset and resend?" before it wipes their answer — so a resend never clobbers a signed response by accident.

Do / Don't

Do

  • Name every row — blank rows count for nothing
  • Check off pulls the same day you make them
  • Flip the 240V flag on 14/2 and 12/2 runs used at 240V
  • Add a panel once and let it create its schedule
  • Use bulk paste for big lists — lead every run with its wire size

Don't

  • Track pulls on paper and "enter them later"
  • Confirm a FILL / RE-FILL on a schedule with hand-typed circuits unless you mean to replace them
  • Delete panels or schedules to clean up the tab
  • Leave a run unnamed and wonder why counts are off
  • Check off runs you haven't pulled yet

Quick answers

I added a run but the counts didn't move.

It needs a name. Unnamed rows don't count anywhere.

My 14/2 run is 240V but it's showing as a 1-pole.

Flip the 240V toggle on the row — it only exists on 14/2 and 12/2. Bigger 240V wire (10/3, 6/3) is a 2-pole automatically, no flag needed.

Where do load-mapping details go that don't fit a row?

The Load Mapping Notes box at the bottom of the tab — free text, write it the way you'd explain it on the phone.

The panel schedule is empty.

New schedules start blank on purpose. Hit FILL to build it from the home runs assigned to that panel.

Why does the tab open on By Floor?

That's the default — it's where adding lives. The By Panel toggle is right there when you're working at a panel.
